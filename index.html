Why this matters

Everything you give comes from somewhere.

The patience your children get. The attention your work gets. The kindness you have left over for yourself at the end of a long day.

All of it is drawn from the same well. And that well is filled at night.

Sleep deeply and you wake with something to give. Sleep badly and you spend the day rationing what little you have — and the people closest to you feel it first.

Being tired is not a character flaw. It is a debt. And it can be repaid.

Deeper sleep is not something you are born with or without. It is something you can learn. That is the whole protocol.

The protocol

Four evenings. Then it's yours.

  1. 01

    Live, online, together

    You join a certified instructor and a small group from home. Nothing to buy, nothing to set up — a screen and a bit of floor is enough. Roughly 90 minutes each session.

  2. 02

    What you actually do

    Breathing exercises, some gentle movement, and a guided meditation. That is the whole method. No chanting, no equipment, no experience needed.

  3. 03

    Every morning after

    Ten minutes on your own. This is the part that changes the mornings — and most people feel the first night's difference before they ever get there.

You keep the practice. You keep the recordings. And because the sessions run online, you can join a group again from anywhere, any time.

Questions

The ten people actually ask

What do I actually get for $149?

Live online sessions with a certified instructor and a small group. The practice itself — breathing, movement and a guided meditation. Recordings you keep. And the option to join a group session again later.

How much time does this take?

A few evenings up front, about 90 minutes each. Then ten minutes a day. If ten minutes a day is genuinely not available, this isn't the right thing to buy.

I've tried meditation. It didn't work.

Most meditation asks you to sit still and do nothing with a mind that won't stop. If yours won't, that's a fair reason to quit.

This is mostly breathing and movement. Your body has something to do, and an instructor is talking you through it live. The guided meditation comes last, once you're already settled. People who have failed at meditation tend to do fine here.

Is this religious?

No. There is nothing to believe and nothing to join. No chanting, no deity, no dress code. It's a breathing technique that came out of a tradition, taught by people who will not ask you what you think about God.

I take sleep medication. I'm seeing someone about my anxiety.
